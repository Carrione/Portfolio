E-commerce · Heureka Group

Heureka.cz & Heureka.sk

08/2021 – 05/2026 · iOS Developer

Heureka.cz Heureka.sk

When I joined the team, the iOS and Android apps were separate, each with its own logic. I was one of two in-house iOS developers, working alongside 2–3 agency developers. During my time there the company decided to go multiplatform — Kotlin Multiplatform was brought in by the partner agency — and most features moved to KMP: shared domain and data layers and, next to the native view models, view models shared by iOS and Android. The UI stayed native — SwiftUI and UIKit on iOS. One codebase thus produces four apps — Heureka.cz and Heureka.sk for iOS and Android.

My contribution

Throughout my time there I continuously developed new features and changes to existing ones based on product requirements — from requirement analysis and solution design through implementation with tests to release on TestFlight and the App Store. On top of that, code reviews and testing other developers' builds. Selected highlights:

Kotlin Multiplatform — I connected the iOS UI to the shared layer, rewrote parts of the code into shared Kotlin and contributed to the Android UI (Jetpack Compose). As the person responsible for the iOS app, I tested and signed off the production release of the KMP version.

Swift 6 — I led the migration of the apps to Swift 6 and strict concurrency: async/await and actors across the codebase, Sendable and @MainActor isolation.

CI/CD and build server — as the sole owner I took over iOS CI/CD on GitLab CI, Fastlane and shell scripts as well as the build server, and rebuilt them several times over the years — tests, pre-production and production builds, and distribution to TestFlight and the App Store. I cut the build time of the app with the shared KMP code from 14 to 3 minutes. We moved the build server to MacStadium and, after an efficiency and cost analysis, back to our own hardware — with faster response times, a return on investment within a year and data fully under our own control.

AI code review — I integrated AI code review built on Claude Code into CI/CD. It posts a comment on every merge request — and with it, a more consistent check of how changes affect code security.

Product comparison — I designed and implemented the feature from solution design to release (see the “product comparison” screenshot in the gallery above). Later I also reworked the look and behaviour of the product detail.

Codebase modernisation — first I cleaned presentation logic and state management out of the views, then rewrote them in SwiftUI (navigation stayed in UIKit). I migrated selected features from REST to GraphQL, introduced local SPM packages and adopted Liquid Glass in the apps.

Buying right in the app — I connected the apps to Heureka's web cart — the cart runs in a web view inside the app, so users can buy without leaving it.

What the apps do

The apps track over 55,000 e-shops, including well-known stores such as Alza, Datart, Allegro, Notino and Pilulka, and together offer millions of products — not only to compare, but also to buy right in the app or at the chosen e-shop.

  • Search — advanced search, barcode scanning and history of both
  • Working with products — saving favourites and comparing products
  • AI shopping advisor — product recommendations based on the user's request
  • Deals — limited-time offers
  • Ratings — reviews of products and e-shops
  • Q&A — product questions with answers from e-shops and an archive of other customers' questions and answers

Architecture — CLEAN

  • Presentation layer — MVI with FlowControllers; next to the native view models also view models shared via KMP
  • Domain layer — repository protocols, use cases, domain logic and models
  • Data layer — repository implementations, data providers, data models

We designed the architecture together with an external company; I enforced it in code review. Before that I had introduced MVP in the presentation layer.
